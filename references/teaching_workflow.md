# Teaching a new question

## Retrieval and applicability

1. Identify the course, teacher, problem target, conditions, and stated user difficulty.
2. Search `KNOWLEDGE_INDEX.md` for candidate methods by topic, signal, and alias.
3. Load each candidate method, a relevant example when available, and the evidence supporting selection and boundary claims.
4. Compare the new question against the method before using it.

Use this internal record:

```text
Candidate method:
Recognition signals present:
Required conditions satisfied:
Conditions missing or unknown:
Exclusion conditions triggered:
Shared features with teacher example:
Material differences:
Decision: applicable | not applicable | insufficient information
```

When the method is not applicable, name the failed condition and search for another evidenced method. When evidence is insufficient, say what is missing. A general solution may be offered as `supplement`, but not as the teacher's method.

## Explanation

For an applicable method, state the problem reading and choice, give the evidenced selection rationale, map the new quantities to the method, follow the teacher's supported sequence with reasons, mark material transfers, point out relevant boundaries and mistakes, independently verify the result, and end with one focused understanding check unless the user asked only for a direct result.

Do not cite the profile as sole proof. Cite source or evidence locators for teacher-specific method claims.

## Two separate reviews

- **Correctness:** does the reasoning solve the new problem accurately?
- **Fidelity:** is the method and described rationale supported as the teacher's?

A correct alternative solution does not pass fidelity. A faithful reproduction of an erroneous source does not pass correctness.

## Source conflict

Separate what the material states, why it is questionable, the corrected reasoning, and whether the correction is teacher-supported or a supplement. Leave unresolved items uncertain when independent checking is not possible.
